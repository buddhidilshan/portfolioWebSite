A useful internal app starts with a clear job to be done. Before arranging controls on a screen, describe what someone needs to accomplish and what currently makes it difficult.

These are design prompts for planning an app, rather than a step-by-step implementation tutorial.

## Follow one real task

Choose a task with a clear beginning and end. For example, a person submits a request, someone reviews it, and the requester receives an outcome. Write down what happens between those points, including the emails, spreadsheets and conversations.

Ask the person doing the work to walk through an ordinary case and an awkward one. The awkward case often reveals requirements that a tidy process diagram misses.

## Decide what the app needs to know

List the information needed to make the next decision. Separate required information from details that are merely convenient to collect.

- Who creates the record?
- Who is responsible for the next step?
- Which statuses have a clear meaning?
- What can be corrected after submission?
- What should happen if information is missing?

Sketch the record before the screen. A screen can be redesigned; unclear ownership and inconsistent statuses tend to spread across the whole workflow.

## Make the next action obvious

Give each screen one primary task. Use labels that match the team's vocabulary and explain the consequence of an action before the user takes it.

A confirmation should say what happened and what comes next. An error should explain what the person can do to recover. Avoid relying on colour alone to communicate either state.

On a phone, check long labels, an open keyboard, validation messages and a record with much more text than the example used during design.

## Try the smallest useful version

Test a small end-to-end journey before adding dashboards or secondary features. Ask someone unfamiliar with the design to complete it without coaching.

Watch where they hesitate. Those moments are evidence about the interface, rather than a reason to add another instruction sheet.

A useful first version lets someone finish their work, understand the result and recover when something goes wrong.
